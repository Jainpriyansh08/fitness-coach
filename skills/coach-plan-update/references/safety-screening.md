# Safety screening, triage and referral

Purpose: decide what can go ahead now, what needs precautions, and what specific activity waits
for a clinician — without blocking a whole plan because of a well-managed condition.
Verify against the current versions of the guidance cited here before relying on details.

## 1. Triage levels

| Level                                                                          | Meaning                                                                                         | What happens                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Ordinary coaching**                                                      | No identified reason to defer low-risk activity                                                 | Normal plan with gradual progression                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **B — Coach with precautions**                                                 | Known, managed conditions, injuries, medicines or special populations                           | Plan goes ahead with specific modifications, monitoring and "stop and tell me" rules; note items to confirm with their clinician when convenient                                                                                 |
| **C — Clinician input needed before specific activities or nutrition changes** | A symptom, uncontrolled condition, recent surgery/injury, or restriction that must be clarified | **Only the affected activities or dietary changes wait** (e.g. no vigorous intervals, no deficit, no loaded spinal work). Everything else that is clearly low-risk can start. Name the professional and the question to ask them |
| **D — Urgent**                                                                 | Possible emergency symptoms                                                                     | No coaching session. Advise immediate medical assessment (India: emergency **112**; ambulance **108** in most states). Coach follows up later                                                                                    |

Rules: "managed condition" ≠ "needs clearance". Don't upgrade to C because a diagnosis sounds
serious; upgrade because something is **uncontrolled, symptomatic, recent, unexplained or
restricted**. Record the level and reason in Coach Notes (`coach_notes.triage`).

## 2. ACSM pre-participation screening (Riebe et al., MSSE 2015; ACSM Guidelines)

Based on three things: (1) do they **currently exercise** regularly (≥ 30 min moderate, ≥ 3
days/week for the past 3 months); (2) **known cardiovascular, metabolic (type 1/type 2
diabetes) or renal disease**, and/or **signs or symptoms** suggesting them; (3) the **desired
intensity**. Risk-factor counting is no longer part of screening.

| Currently exercising? | Disease / symptoms                                | ACSM recommendation                                           |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| No                    | No disease, no symptoms                           | No clearance needed; start light–moderate, progress gradually |
| No                    | Known CV/metabolic/renal disease, **no symptoms** | Medical clearance _recommended_; then light–moderate          |
| No                    | **Any symptoms**                                  | Medical clearance recommended before starting                 |
| Yes                   | No disease, no symptoms                           | Continue; progress as tolerated                               |
| Yes                   | Known disease, no symptoms                        | Continue moderate; clearance recommended before **vigorous**  |
| Yes                   | **Any symptoms**                                  | Stop exercise; seek medical clearance                         |

**Signs/symptoms list (ACSM):** pain or discomfort in the chest, neck, jaw, arms or other areas
that may be ischaemic; shortness of breath at rest or with mild exertion; dizziness or fainting;
breathlessness lying down or waking at night breathless; ankle swelling; palpitations or racing
heart; calf pain with walking that eases with rest (claudication); known heart murmur; unusual
fatigue or breathlessness with usual activities.

**Where guidance differs (a Decision-Brief item):** for asymptomatic, sedentary people with
diabetes, the ADA position statement (Colberg et al., _Diabetes Care_ 2016) says pre-exercise
medical clearance is **not necessary** to begin low- or moderate-intensity activity not exceeding
brisk walking or everyday living, and that clearance is advised before higher-intensity exercise
or for those at high cardiovascular risk. The default for this skill: **triage B** — start
walking/light-moderate strength with precautions, recommend they mention the new programme at
their next diabetes review, and require clearance before vigorous work. Tell the coach that ACSM
is more conservative for sedentary people with known metabolic disease, so the coach decides.

Hypertension alone is not "cardiovascular disease" in this screening model, but uncontrolled or
very high blood pressure is a C/D item (below).

## 3. Red flags — triage D (urgent, today)

- Chest pain, pressure or tightness (at rest or with exertion), especially spreading to arm, jaw,
  neck or back, or with sweating, nausea or breathlessness
- Fainting or near-fainting, especially during or just after exercise
- Severe or sudden breathlessness; breathlessness at rest
- Stroke signs: face drooping, arm/leg weakness, speech difficulty, sudden vision loss, confusion
- Sudden, severe "worst-ever" headache; headache with fever and stiff neck
- Resting BP around 180/120 or higher, or high BP with chest pain, breathlessness, severe headache,
  vision change or confusion
- One-sided calf pain/swelling with breathlessness or chest pain (possible clot)
- Back pain with numbness in the saddle/groin area, new bladder or bowel problems, or weakness in
  both legs (cauda equina warning signs)
- Severe abdominal pain; vomiting blood; black stools
- Pregnancy: vaginal bleeding, fluid leaking, regular painful contractions, chest pain, severe
  headache, calf pain/swelling, dizziness, reduced baby movements
- Postpartum: heavy bleeding, fever, severe pain, breathlessness, calf pain, thoughts of harming
  self or baby
- Severe low blood sugar symptoms not improving with fast sugar (confusion, inability to swallow)
- Thoughts of suicide or self-harm → India: **Tele-MANAS 14416** (or 1-800-891-4416), 24/7, free;
  emergency 112

## 4. Clinician-input triggers (triage C), by area

| Area                   | Trigger                                                                                                                                   | Defer until cleared                                                                     | Can go ahead                                                                                           |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Heart / circulation    | Any ACSM symptom (above); known heart disease without cardiac-rehab/doctor guidance; recent cardiac event or procedure                    | All structured exercise beyond normal daily walking                                     | Education, nutrition habits, sleep                                                                     |
| Blood pressure         | Readings ≥ 160/100 or labelled uncontrolled; dizziness on standing                                                                        | Vigorous intervals, heavy/maximal lifting, breath-holding efforts                       | Light–moderate walking/cycling, light strength with steady breathing (if doctor hasn't said otherwise) |
| Diabetes               | Frequent hypos; on insulin/sulfonylureas with no hypo plan; foot ulcers; proliferative retinopathy; neuropathy with balance/foot problems | Fasted training; heavy straining/Valsalva (retinopathy); weight-bearing on wounded feet | Supervised light–moderate activity with hypo precautions; non-weight-bearing options                   |
| Thyroid                | Hyperthyroid symptoms (racing heart, tremor, heat intolerance, weight loss) or recently diagnosed/changing treatment                      | Vigorous and high-heart-rate training                                                   | Light mobility, walking, gentle strength                                                               |
| Kidney / liver disease | Chronic kidney disease, dialysis, liver disease                                                                                           | Protein targets, supplements (creatine, high-dose anything), potassium-rich remedies    | Activity per doctor; habits                                                                            |
| Musculoskeletal        | Recent surgery; new injury; pain that is worsening, at night, or with swelling/locking/giving way; numbness/tingling/weakness             | The affected movements and loading                                                      | Training of unaffected areas; tolerated movements                                                      |
| Pregnancy              | Any pregnancy without obstetric provider's go-ahead; any complication                                                                     | Structured programme                                                                    | General guidance per provider                                                                          |
| Postpartum             | < 6–8 weeks, caesarean wound concerns, pelvic-floor symptoms, doming/pain, heavy bleeding                                                 | Impact, heavy lifting, intense core and HIIT                                            | Walking, breathing, gentle mobility, pelvic-floor awareness (per provider/physio)                      |
| Respiratory            | Asthma that is uncontrolled (frequent reliever use, night symptoms)                                                                       | Hard conditioning, cold/polluted outdoor sessions                                       | Indoor moderate work with inhaler as prescribed                                                        |
| Eating / mental health | Signs of an eating disorder; severe depression; panic with exercise                                                                       | Calorie deficits, weighing, food tracking                                               | Gentle activity, regular meals, support                                                                |
| Unexplained            | Unexplained weight loss, fever, night sweats, persistent fatigue, new breathlessness                                                      | Hard training                                                                           | Light activity; urge GP review                                                                         |

## 5. Referral map

| Need                                                                                                                               | Professional                                                                          |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Chest symptoms, BP, diabetes, thyroid, medicines, lab tests, unexplained symptoms                                                  | General physician / their treating doctor (endocrinologist, cardiologist as relevant) |
| Pain, injury, return after surgery, movement restriction                                                                           | Physiotherapist (orthopaedic/sports); orthopaedic surgeon via GP if needed            |
| Leaking, pelvic heaviness/pain, abdominal doming after pregnancy                                                                   | Pelvic-health (women's health) physiotherapist; gynaecologist/obstetrician            |
| Pregnancy                                                                                                                          | Obstetrician / gynaecologist / midwife                                                |
| Complex nutrition (CKD, eating disorder, IBD, food allergy, gestational diabetes, type 1 diabetes carb-insulin matching, children) | Registered dietitian (RD) and the treating doctor                                     |
| Low mood, anxiety, eating disorder, trauma                                                                                         | Psychologist / psychiatrist; Tele-MANAS 14416 for support                             |
| Sleep apnoea signs (loud snoring, gasping, daytime sleepiness)                                                                     | GP / sleep specialist                                                                 |

Write referrals as a concrete question the client can take to the professional, e.g. _"I'm
starting a strength programme 3×/week with light weights and brisk walking. Is there any
activity or intensity I should avoid with my blood pressure readings?"_

## 6. Medication awareness (for coaching decisions — never advise on the medicine itself)

| Medicine type (examples)                                 | What it means for coaching                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Insulin, sulfonylureas (glimepiride, gliclazide)         | Hypoglycaemia risk with exercise, fasting, long gaps, or glucose-lowering remedies (methi, karela, cinnamon, berberine, ACV). Don't train fasted without their doctor's plan; carry fast sugar; know hypo signs                                      |
| Metformin                                                | Low hypo risk alone; GI effects; long-term B12 deficiency possible — doctors monitor B12                                                                                                                                                             |
| SGLT2 inhibitors (dapagliflozin, empagliflozin)          | Dehydration; rare ketoacidosis risk that rises with very-low-carb/keto diets and fasting — never put these clients on keto/very-low-carb or long fasts without their doctor                                                                          |
| GLP-1 / GIP-GLP-1 medicines (semaglutide, tirzepatide)   | Low appetite, nausea → small protein-first meals, hydration; resistance training + adequate protein to protect muscle during fast weight loss; hypos only if combined with insulin/sulfonylureas                                                     |
| Beta-blockers (metoprolol, propranolol)                  | Blunted heart rate → use RPE/talk test, not heart-rate zones; may reduce exercise capacity                                                                                                                                                           |
| Diuretics                                                | Dehydration, cramps, electrolyte shifts → hydration; check with doctor before electrolyte or potassium-rich remedies                                                                                                                                 |
| ACE inhibitors / ARBs (ramipril, telmisartan)            | Dizziness on standing → slow transitions, longer cool-down; potassium supplements/salt substitutes only with doctor OK                                                                                                                               |
| Levothyroxine (thyroxine)                                | Taken on an empty stomach; calcium, iron, magnesium, soy, coffee and high-fibre remedies can reduce absorption if taken too close — keep the doctor's timing; separate supplements as the doctor/pharmacist advises (commonly ~4 h for calcium/iron) |
| Blood thinners (warfarin, apixaban), antiplatelets       | Bruising/bleeding; caution with high-dose fish oil, turmeric/curcumin, ginger, garlic supplements, fenugreek; warfarin + big changes in green leafy veg (vitamin K) → keep intake consistent, tell doctor                                            |
| Statins                                                  | Report new muscle pain/weakness to the doctor; don't blame it on training without checking                                                                                                                                                           |
| Steroids (prednisolone)                                  | Higher glucose, appetite, bone and muscle effects                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| Antidepressants, antipsychotics, some epilepsy medicines | Weight gain and appetite changes are medication effects — never framed as a willpower failure                                                                                                                                                        |
| ADHD stimulants                                          | Reduced daytime appetite, raised heart rate → protein at breakfast/evening, hydration                                                                                                                                                                |
| Oral contraceptives, HRT                                 | No change to programming; never comment on the prescription                                                                                                                                                                                          |

## 7. Coach scope

**A coach can:** screen and refer; programme exercise around confirmed restrictions; teach
technique; build habits; give general healthy-eating guidance and Indian meal structures; set
adjustable energy/protein estimates for generally healthy people; suggest low-risk, evidence-graded
food-based remedies and common supplements with cautions; support medical advice (e.g. hypo
precautions); track and report symptoms to the client's clinician.

**A coach must not:** diagnose; interpret tests as a doctor would; treat pain or injuries as
rehab without a clinician; set therapeutic diets (CKD, ED, IBD, type 1 insulin matching);
prescribe or change medicines or doses; promise to "fix" a condition; pressure anyone to share
health details.

## 8. How to word it

- To the coach: "This sounds like something her GP should look at before we add intervals —
  until then, walking and the light strength sessions can go ahead."
- To the client: "That's worth checking with your doctor — not because exercise is dangerous for
  you, but so we can push harder safely. Until then, here's what we'll do…"
- Never: "You have X", "This exercise is unsafe for you", "Stop your medicine", "This will cure…".
