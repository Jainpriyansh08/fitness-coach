# Training design

Goal: the **most effective plan this person will actually do** — simple, progressive, safe for
their body, and resilient to busy weeks. Don't pick advanced programming to look professional.

## 1. Baseline guidelines

- **WHO 2020 (adults):** 150–300 min/week moderate or 75–150 min vigorous aerobic activity (or a
  mix), plus muscle-strengthening for all major muscle groups on ≥ 2 days/week; reduce sitting;
  older adults add balance/multicomponent training ≥ 3 days/week. _Some is better than none._
- **Steps:** ~7,000/day is associated with large health benefits vs ~2,000, with smaller extra gain
  up to 10,000 (Ding 2025). Set step targets from the client's current baseline (+1,000–2,000/day
  steps), not a universal 10k.
- **Resistance training (ACSM):** novices — each major muscle group 2–3×/week, 1–3 sets of
  ~8–15 reps, moderate effort, emphasise technique. More volume helps hypertrophy up to a point,
  but adherence and recovery limit it for most clients.

## 2. Frequency and split

Choose from **realistic** days (the busiest-week answer), not the hoped-for number.

| Realistic days | Recommended structure                                                                                  | Why                                                          |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1–2            | Full-body A/B (+ daily walks)                                                                          | Each session covers everything; missing one isn't a disaster |
| 3              | Full-body A/B/C or A/B alternating                                                                     | Each muscle ~3×/week, simplest to progress                   |
| 4              | Full-body ×4 (beginners) or Upper/Lower ×2                                                             | Upper/lower suits intermediates who want more volume         |
| 5–6            | Upper/Lower + 1–2 optional (conditioning/arms/mobility), or PPL only for experienced clients with time | High frequency rarely survives busy weeks for beginners      |

Rules: avoid hard sessions for the same muscles on consecutive days; give 1–2 full rest days;
start beginners at **2–3** strength days even if they want more (weeks 1–2), then add.
Always include: a **Busy Day** 10–20 min fallback and a **mobility/walk** day option.

## 3. Exercise selection

Cover the movement patterns each week: **squat · hinge · single-leg/lunge · horizontal push ·
vertical push · horizontal pull · vertical pull · carry · core (anti-extension / anti-rotation /
lateral)** + aerobic work.

Choose the variation that is, in order:

1. **Tolerated** — pain-free or within the agreed pain rule for this client.
2. **Effective** — loads the target muscles through a useful range; can be progressed.
3. **Accessible** — uses equipment they actually have, can be learned in one session.
4. **Enjoyable** — or at least not hated (dislikes kill adherence).

Prefer stable, easy-to-learn options for beginners (machines, dumbbells, bodyweight, bands) over
complex barbell lifts; add barbells when technique, interest and supervision allow.
Every exercise row in the workbook needs: key cues, sets × reps per phase, rest, effort, an
**easier option**, a **harder option**, **"if it bothers you"** guidance, and a video
(`exercise-library.md` first). Limit beginners to ~5–7 exercises per 40-minute session.

**Environment menus**

- _Home, no equipment:_ squat to chair, split squat, glute bridge, hip hinge with backpack, incline
  → knee → floor push-up, towel door row*, lying Y-raise, plank variations, dead bug, step-ups on
  stairs, wall sit. (*check door safety: pull toward the side that closes into the frame.)
- _Home, dumbbells/bands:_ goblet squat, DB Romanian deadlift, reverse lunge, floor press, one-arm
  row, band pulldown, overhead press (if shoulders OK), suitcase carry.
- _Gym:_ leg press/box squat/goblet squat, RDL/hip thrust, lat pulldown, seated cable row, chest
  press/DB bench, shoulder press machine, leg curl, carries; cardio machines.
- _Park/terrace:_ bench step-ups, incline push-ups, pole/railing towel rows, walking lunges,
  brisk walk/hill walks.
- _Travel/hotel:_ the Busy Day circuit; bodyweight versions; hotel gym substitutions.

## 4. Sets, reps, effort, rest

| Client                      | Sets per exercise | Reps                             | Effort                       | Rest                |
| --------------------------- | ----------------- | -------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------- |
| New / returning (weeks 1–2) | 2                 | 8–15                             | 3–4 reps in reserve (RPE ~6) | 60–90 s             |
| Novice (weeks 3+)           | 2–3               | 8–12 (compounds 6–10 when ready) | 2–3 RIR (RPE ~7–8)           | 60–120 s            |
| Intermediate                | 3–4               | 5–12 by exercise                 | 1–3 RIR on main lifts        | 90–180 s main lifts |

Explain RIR to clients as "stop with 2–3 good reps still in the tank". Core and mobility work:
controlled reps or holds, stop before form breaks.

## 5. Progression and regression

- **Double progression:** work up the rep range on all sets → then add a little load or move to the
  next harder version → reps reset. Progress **one variable at a time**.
- **Readiness check (all true):** technique solid · all sets at top of range · effort still 2–3
  RIR · no new or increased symptoms · recovering by next day · ≥ 2 weeks consistency.
- **Home progression ladder (fixed weights):** more reps → extra set (max 3–4) → slower lowering
  (3 s) → pauses → harder variation (e.g. bed → floor push-ups; split squat → rear-foot elevated).
- **Regress** when: pain beyond the agreed rule, technique breaks, poor sleep/illness, symptoms,
  missed weeks. Regression is normal — say so.
- **Lighter week** (one set fewer, same exercises) after illness, travel, festival weeks, several
  poor-sleep days, or every 4–8 weeks if fatigue builds. Don't schedule deloads mechanically for
  beginners who aren't fatigued.

## 6. Cardio, steps and conditioning

- Default: walking (steps + post-meal walks) and 1–2 moderate cardio sessions if time allows.
- Intensity by **talk test / RPE** (essential for beta-blocker users): easy = full conversation;
  moderate = sentences; vigorous = a few words.
- HIIT: optional, only for clients cleared for vigorous work who enjoy it; not a default for
  beginners, postpartum, uncontrolled BP/thyroid, or joint pain. Low-impact intervals (cycle,
  incline walk) first.

## 7. Warm-up, cool-down, mobility

- Warm-up 5 min: 2 min general (march, cycle, brisk walk) + 3–4 moves rehearsing the session
  (cat-cow, glute bridge, bodyweight squat, arm circles, band pull-aparts) + 1 light set of the
  first exercise.
- Cool-down 3–5 min: easy walking, 2–3 comfortable stretches, slow breathing (longer if on BP
  medicines).
- Mobility day: 15–20 min routine for desk stiffness (hips, thoracic spine, shoulders) + walk.

## 8. Busy weeks and the minimum effective week

- **Busy Day session:** 10–20 min, 4–5 moves, 2–3 rounds — counts as a session.
- **Minimum week:** 2 short sessions + daily walking = success. Missed day → do the next planned
  session; never "double up".
- Travel: plan the minimum week in advance; hotel/bodyweight versions.

## 9. Special considerations

- **Pain-aware programming:** agree a pain rule (e.g. ≤ 3/10 that settles by next morning); swap or
  shorten the range rather than stopping all training; log symptoms in the Check-in Tracker.
- **Medicines:** beta-blockers → RPE; BP medicines → slow position changes, longer cool-down;
  insulin/sulfonylureas → hypo precautions; see safety-screening.md §6.
- **Menstrual cycle:** research shows performance changes across the cycle are small and
  individual; rigid "cycle syncing" isn't supported as a requirement. Offer flexibility (lighter
  days when symptoms are bad) — a Decision-Brief item if the client or coach wants cycle-based
  programming.
- **India-specific:** heat/humidity (train early/late or indoors, more fluids/electrolytes); monsoon
  (indoor backups); high-AQI days (indoor sessions, especially with asthma); festival and wedding
  seasons (plan minimum weeks); gym crowding (time-efficient supersets, machine alternatives);
  women training at home (privacy, clothing, space — choose moves that fit).
- **Never:** prescribe exercise as treatment for an injury; push through red-flag symptoms; use
  punishment workouts for food.

## 10. Explaining the choice

For the coach (Coach Notes): why this split vs. plausible alternatives (e.g. "full-body 3×
instead of PPL: she can realistically train 3 days, missing one PPL day would skip a whole
pattern for a week"). For the client (session tab "Objective"): one sentence on what the session
builds and why it suits them.
