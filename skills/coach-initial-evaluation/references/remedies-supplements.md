# Home remedies & supplements — pre-graded cards

These are **starting points**. Before suggesting any item to a client: check it against their
conditions, medicines, pregnancy/breastfeeding status and allergies; re-check the evidence with a
quick search (new trials, retractions, safety notices); then put it in the Decision Brief.

Principles:

- **Food and behaviour first.** A remedy is optional and never replaces medical treatment.
- **Profile-specific.** Each item needs a reason _for this client_ — no default "stack".
- **One new thing at a time**, with a way to judge whether it helps (and when to stop).
- **Never** frame a remedy as a cure, "detox", "fat burner" or hormone fix.
- **Medication check** whenever the client takes medicine for blood sugar, blood pressure, thyroid,
  blood thinning, mood, or has kidney/liver disease.
- **Quality:** supplements in India are regulated by FSSAI as foods. Prefer brands with published
  third-party lab reports; label inaccuracies and "amino spiking" in protein powders have been
  reported. Never name or push specific brands unless the coach asks.

## A. Kitchen remedies

### Fenugreek (methi) seeds / methi water

- **Claim:** lowers blood sugar; helps weight loss, digestion.
- **Evidence:** **Moderate** for blood glucose — meta-analysis (Neelakantan 2014, 10 trials):
  lower fasting and 2-hour glucose and HbA1c, mainly in people _with diabetes_ at medium–high doses
  (≈ ≥ 5 g/day seed powder); most trials low quality. **Limited** for weight loss.
- **Use:** 1–2 tsp (≈ 5–10 g) seeds soaked overnight in a glass of water; drink the water and
  chew the seeds, or add powder/seeds to dal, sabzi, roti dough. Judge by fasting/post-meal sugar
  readings over 4–8 weeks _if the client already monitors them_.
- **Cautions:** adds to glucose-lowering medicines (insulin, sulfonylureas) → hypoglycaemia risk —
  doctor check first; avoid medicinal amounts in pregnancy; gas, bloating, loose stools; maple-syrup
  smell in sweat/urine (harmless); possible allergy in people allergic to peanuts/chickpeas
  (legume family); may affect bleeding/warfarin — doctor check if on blood thinners; its fibre can
  slow absorption of medicines — keep away from the thyroid tablet and other medicines (≥ 1 h, or as
  the pharmacist advises).
- **Alternative:** 10–15 min walk after meals; fibre-first meals; protein with carbs.

### Methi + dhania (coriander) + saunf (fennel) seed water (common combination)

- **Evidence:** only the **fenugreek** part has meaningful human evidence (above). Coriander:
  **Limited** (mostly animal studies on glucose). Fennel: **Traditional/Limited** (digestion,
  bloating). The combination itself hasn't been studied.
- **Fair framing:** "a low-calorie morning drink that may help a little with blood sugar (from the
  methi) and digestion — not a treatment." Useful if it replaces sugary tea/juice.
- **Cautions:** methi cautions above; fennel — avoid concentrated fennel oil/strong teas in
  pregnancy and breastfeeding (estragole concerns), caution with oestrogen-sensitive conditions;
  diabetes medicines → doctor check.

### Jeera (cumin) water

- **Evidence:** **Limited** — a few small trials (cumin powder with food) suggest modest weight/
  lipid effects; low certainty. **Traditional** for digestion.
- **Cautions:** food amounts are safe; may add slightly to glucose-lowering medicines.
- **Fair framing:** fine as a habit drink; don't expect fat loss from it.

### Saunf (fennel) water / chewing saunf after meals

- **Evidence:** **Traditional/Limited** for bloating and digestion.
- **Cautions:** as above (pregnancy/breastfeeding: avoid concentrated forms; oestrogen-sensitive
  conditions).

### Dhania (coriander seed) water

- **Evidence:** **Limited** (animal data for glucose and cholesterol; very little human data).
- **Cautions:** generally safe in food amounts; caution with glucose-lowering medicines.

### Apple cider vinegar (ACV)

- **Claim:** lowers blood sugar, burns fat, "alkalises", detoxes.
- **Evidence:** **Moderate** for a _small_ reduction in after-meal glucose and insulin when taken
  with a carbohydrate meal (Shishehbor 2017 meta-analysis; small, short trials). **Limited** for
  weight loss — a widely publicised 2024 weight-loss trial (BMJ Nutrition, Prevention & Health)
  was **retracted in 2025**; don't cite it. "Alkalising" and "detox" claims: **Not supported**.
- **Use (if chosen):** 1 tbsp (15 ml) diluted in a full glass of water, with or just before the
  biggest carb meal; drink through a straw and rinse the mouth after.
- **Cautions:** acidity/GERD, gastritis or ulcers → avoid; tooth-enamel erosion (never neat, never
  sipped all day); delays stomach emptying — avoid with gastroparesis (some people with diabetes);
  large amounts can lower potassium — doctor check with diuretics, insulin or digoxin; ACV
  gummies/tablets are not equivalent and tablets have caused throat injury.
- **Alternative:** post-meal walk, vegetables first, protein + fibre with carbs (similar or larger
  effect on after-meal sugar, no side effects).

### Honey + salt (± lemon) before a workout

- **Claim:** "natural pre-workout" — energy and hydration.
- **Evidence:** the combination itself is untested. Its parts: carbohydrate before/during exercise
  improves performance in **longer (> 60–90 min) or fasted** sessions — **Strong**; for a 45–60 min
  gym session after normal meals, benefit is small or none. Honey works like other sugars (≈ 17 g
  carbs and ≈ 64 kcal per tbsp). Sodium helps fluid retention for **heavy sweaters, hot/humid
  conditions or long sessions** — **Moderate**; little need for short sessions in mild conditions.
  Overall for typical clients: **Limited**.
- **Good fit:** early-morning sessions on an empty stomach where the client feels weak; long
  sessions, sport, or hot/humid outdoor training; heavy sweaters with salty sweat marks.
- **Use:** 1 tsp–1 tbsp honey + a small pinch of salt (± lemon) in 250–300 ml water, 15–30 min
  before training.
- **Cautions:** diabetes — adds fast sugar (may be _part_ of a hypo-prevention plan for insulin/
  sulfonylurea users, but only per their doctor's plan); high blood pressure, heart failure or
  kidney disease — extra sodium → doctor check; adds calories in a fat-loss phase (small, but
  unnecessary for short sessions); never give honey to infants under 1 year.
- **Alternatives:** a banana, 2–3 dates, or a small normal snack 60–90 min before; nimbu pani
  (lemon-salt-sugar water) or ORS for heat; plain water for short, mild sessions.

### Lemon water / warm water in the morning

- **Evidence:** hydration habit — fine. "Burns fat", "detoxes", "alkalises": **Not supported**.
- **Cautions:** enamel erosion if sipped all day (rinse with water); reflux.

### Cinnamon (dalchini)

- **Evidence:** **Limited/mixed** — some meta-analyses show small fasting-glucose reductions, HbA1c
  effects inconsistent.
- **Cautions:** cassia cinnamon (most common in markets) contains coumarin, which can harm the liver
  at high regular intake — keep to culinary amounts or prefer Ceylon cinnamon; caution with
  glucose-lowering medicines and blood thinners.

### Amla (Indian gooseberry)

- **Evidence:** **Limited** (small trials on lipids/glucose). Excellent vitamin C food.
- **Use:** as fruit, chutney or murabba (watch sugar in murabba); concentrated supplements → check
  with doctor if on blood thinners or glucose-lowering medicines.

### Ginger (adrak)

- **Evidence:** **Moderate** for nausea (including pregnancy nausea, within usual food amounts);
  **Limited–Moderate** for reducing exercise muscle soreness.
- **Cautions:** high-dose supplements with blood thinners; reflux in some people.

### Turmeric (haldi) / curcumin

- **Evidence:** food amounts — safe, culinary. Curcumin supplements — **Limited–Moderate** for knee
  osteoarthritis pain; poorly absorbed without piperine/formulations.
- **Cautions:** supplements (especially with piperine/black pepper extract) have been linked to
  liver injury (NIH LiverTox); blood thinners; gallstones; pregnancy (supplements). Haldi doodh is
  fine as food.

### Chia seeds / sabja (basil) seeds

- **Evidence:** fibre and satiety — **Limited** for weight loss specifically; chia provides plant
  omega-3 (ALA).
- **Cautions:** always soak (choking risk if swallowed dry, especially sabja); increase water;
  bloating if fibre is increased quickly.

### Karela (bitter gourd) juice / jamun seed powder

- **Evidence:** **Limited** for blood glucose (small, inconsistent trials).
- **Cautions:** hypoglycaemia with diabetes medicines; stomach upset; avoid karela in pregnancy;
  karela seeds can trigger haemolysis in G6PD deficiency. As vegetables in meals — fine.

### Ajwain (carom seeds)

- **Evidence:** **Traditional** for gas/bloating. Food amounts safe; avoid concentrated oils in
  pregnancy.

### Triphala

- **Evidence:** **Limited**; acts mainly as a mild laxative.
- **Cautions:** pregnancy/breastfeeding; diarrhoea; may interact with some medicines. Constipation
  is better addressed first with fibre, water, activity and routine.

### Soaked almonds / walnuts

- **Evidence:** nuts in the diet — **Strong** association with better heart health; soaking claims
  — **Limited**.
- **Use:** a small handful (≈ 20–30 g ≈ 120–180 kcal) — portion matters in fat-loss phases.

### Ghee / coconut oil

- **Evidence:** fine in moderate amounts as part of cooking; no fat-burning or "metabolism"
  effect; both are calorie-dense (≈ 45 kcal/tsp). Coconut oil raises LDL more than most vegetable
  oils.

### Black coffee before training

- **Evidence:** caffeine improves performance — **Strong** (ISSN 2021: typically 3–6 mg/kg; lower
  doses ≈ 1–2 mg/kg, about one cup, also help some people).
- **Cautions:** sleep — coffee within ~8–9 hours of bed reduces sleep (Gardiner 2023); anxiety,
  palpitations, reflux; hyperthyroid symptoms; pregnancy (keep total caffeine ≤ 200 mg/day);
  high BP (short-term rise). Don't stack with pre-workout powders.

### "Detox" teas, juices, cleanses, laxative or diuretic slimming products

- **Evidence:** **Not supported** — weight change is water/bowel content; risks include
  dehydration, electrolyte problems and medicine interactions; some contain senna or undeclared
  drugs. Recommend against.

## B. Supplements

### Protein powder (whey / plant)

- **Evidence:** **Strong** that total daily protein matters (1.4–2.0 g/kg for exercising people,
  ISSN 2017); powder is a convenience, not a requirement.
- **When:** client can't reach the protein target with food (common in vegetarian diets).
- **Cautions:** lactose intolerance → whey isolate or plant blends; kidney disease → doctor/RD sets
  protein; choose third-party-tested products; count it as part of meals, not extra calories.

### Creatine monohydrate

- **Evidence:** **Strong** for strength and lean mass with resistance training (ISSN 2017), in
  women and men, younger and older adults.
- **Use:** 3–5 g/day, any time, no loading needed.
- **Cautions:** 0.5–2 kg water-weight gain early (warn clients watching the scale); kidney disease
  → avoid unless the doctor agrees; raises blood creatinine a little — tell the doctor before kidney
  tests; pregnancy/breastfeeding — insufficient data.

### Caffeine (tablets / pre-workout powders)

- See black coffee. Powders often contain 150–300 mg plus other stimulants — avoid with heart
  rhythm problems, hypertension, anxiety, hyperthyroidism, pregnancy.

### Vitamin D3

- **Evidence:** **Strong** for correcting deficiency; deficiency is common in India (indoor work,
  skin cover, pollution).
- **Use:** test first when possible; deficiency doses (e.g. weekly 60,000 IU sachets) only as a
  doctor prescribes; general maintenance doses are modest (commonly 600–2,000 IU/day; adult
  tolerable upper level 4,000 IU/day, US IOM).
- **Cautions:** toxicity from self-dosed megadoses happens — high calcium, kidney problems; kidney
  stones, sarcoidosis, kidney disease → doctor.

### Vitamin B12

- **Evidence:** **Strong** for correcting deficiency. At risk: vegetarians/vegans, people on
  metformin long-term, acid-suppressing medicines, older adults.
- **Use:** test when possible; doctor guides dose. Low risk.

### Iron

- **Evidence:** **Strong** for treating iron deficiency. Anaemia is very common in Indian women
  (NFHS-5: ~57% of women 15–49).
- **Use:** **only after a blood test** and on a doctor's advice; don't give to men or
  post-menopausal women without testing.
- **Cautions:** constipation, nausea; separate from tea/coffee, calcium and levothyroxine (commonly
  by ≥ 4 h for the thyroid tablet — follow the doctor/pharmacist). Food first: dals, chana, rajma,
  green leafy veg, ragi, eggs, meat/fish; pair plant iron with vitamin C (lemon, amla, tomato); keep
  tea/coffee away from iron-rich meals. Jaggery is mostly sugar — not a meaningful iron fix.

### Calcium

- **Use:** food first — milk, curd, paneer, ragi, til, green leafy veg. Supplements only if intake is
  low or a doctor advises (e.g. osteoporosis).
- **Cautions:** separate from levothyroxine and iron; constipation; kidney stones (supplements,
  not food calcium).

### Omega-3 (fish oil / algal oil)

- **Evidence:** **Limited–Moderate** for general health in non-fish-eaters; triglyceride lowering at
  prescription doses is a medical decision.
- **Cautions:** marine omega-3 supplements are associated with higher atrial fibrillation risk,
  more so above 1 g/day (Gencer 2021) — avoid high doses without a doctor, especially with heart
  rhythm history; blood thinners; fishy burps. Vegetarians: algal oil; food sources: walnuts,
  flax, chia (ALA, limited conversion).

### Magnesium

- **Evidence:** **Limited** for sleep, cramps and anxiety in people without deficiency.
- **Cautions:** kidney disease → avoid unless prescribed; diarrhoea (oxide/citrate more than
  glycinate); separate from levothyroxine and some antibiotics.
- **Food first:** nuts, seeds, whole grains, dals, green leafy veg.

### Multivitamin

- **Evidence:** **Not supported** for most people eating varied diets; may be reasonable for very
  restricted diets, very low calorie intake, or appetite-suppressing medicines — coach's call.

### Electrolytes / ORS

- **Use:** heavy sweating, long sessions, heat/humidity, diarrhoea (ORS). Short gym sessions: water.
- **Cautions:** sodium/potassium with high BP, heart failure, kidney disease, certain BP medicines.

### Psyllium husk (isabgol)

- **Evidence:** **Moderate–Strong** soluble fibre benefits: lower LDL cholesterol, improved glycaemic
  control, constipation relief, fullness.
- **Use:** 1 tsp in a full glass of water, increase slowly to 1–2 tsp once or twice daily.
- **Cautions:** always with plenty of water (choking/obstruction risk if dry); take medicines ≥ 2 h
  apart (including levothyroxine); bloating at first; swallowing difficulties → avoid.

### Ashwagandha

- **Evidence:** **Moderate–Limited** for perceived stress and sleep (several small RCTs); **Limited**
  for strength/testosterone claims.
- **Cautions:** liver injury cases reported, including rare severe ones (NIH LiverTox); can raise
  thyroid hormone levels — avoid with hyperthyroidism and check with the doctor if on
  levothyroxine; avoid in pregnancy/breastfeeding; may add to sedatives; autoimmune conditions →
  doctor. Several European regulators have restricted or advised against it (Denmark banned it in
  supplements in 2023). Usually a **don't include** unless the coach and client's doctor agree.

### Berberine

- **Evidence:** **Moderate** for lowering glucose and lipids (meta-analyses of mostly small trials);
  "nature's Ozempic" claims: **Not supported**.
- **Cautions:** GI upset; many drug interactions (it inhibits drug-metabolising enzymes); adds to
  diabetes medicines (hypoglycaemia); avoid in pregnancy/breastfeeding. Doctor decision.

### Inositol (myo-inositol) for PCOS

- **Evidence:** **Limited** — the evidence review behind the 2023 international PCOS guideline found
  it limited and inconclusive (possible metabolic benefits; fewer GI side effects than metformin).
- **Use:** only as a shared decision with the client's doctor; not a replacement for lifestyle
  or medical care.

### Probiotics

- **Evidence:** **Limited** and strain-specific; not a weight-loss tool. Food sources (curd,
  buttermilk, idli/dosa batter) are fine.

### Collagen

- **Evidence:** **Limited** (small effects on skin/joint pain in some trials). Incomplete protein —
  counts only partly toward the protein target.

### BCAA / EAA

- **Evidence:** **Not supported** when daily protein is adequate. Spend the money on food protein.

### Glucosamine / chondroitin

- **Evidence:** **Limited/Not supported** for knee osteoarthritis in major guidelines; exercise and
  weight management have stronger evidence.

### "Fat burners", thermogenics, keto pills, Garcinia, green tea extract capsules

- **Evidence:** **Not supported**; liver injury reports with several (NIH LiverTox); undeclared
  stimulants in some products. Recommend against.
