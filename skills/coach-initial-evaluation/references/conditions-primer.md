## Metabolic

### Prediabetes & type 2 diabetes

- **Ask:** diagnosis year; latest HbA1c/fasting sugar if they know it (don't interpret beyond
  "in range per your doctor?"); medicines (insulin/sulfonylureas → hypo risk; SGLT2 → no keto);
  hypo episodes; feet (numbness, wounds), eyes (retinopathy), kidneys; does the doctor know about
  the new programme?
- **Training:** both aerobic and resistance training improve glucose control; combine them
  (ACSM 2022 / ADA): aim toward ≥ 150 min/week moderate activity over ≥ 3 days with no more than 2
  days in a row without activity, plus 2–3 resistance sessions; break up sitting; **10–15 min walks
  after meals** blunt post-meal glucose (Buffey 2022). Hypo precautions for insulin/sulfonylurea
  users (snack available, don't train fasted without their doctor's plan). Neuropathy → foot care,
  good shoes, non-weight-bearing options if foot wounds; proliferative retinopathy → avoid heavy
  straining/breath-holding and high-intensity work until the eye doctor clears it.
- **Nutrition:** no single ideal macro split; reduce sugary drinks and refined carbs; fibre and
  vegetables first; protein with every meal; smaller rice/roti portions spread across the day;
  whole fruit in portions is fine; weight loss of ~5–7% (prediabetes) or more (type 2) improves
  control and can induce remission in some (doctor-led).
- **Watch for:** hypo signs (shaky, sweaty, confused, very hungry) → fast sugar, stop, tell coach;
  very high readings with symptoms → doctor.
- **Myths:** "no rice/fruit ever", "karela/methi cures diabetes", "sweat out sugar", "insulin
  means you failed".
- **Coach vs clinician:** coach — activity, meal structure, habits; clinician — medicines, targets,
  lab interpretation, complications.

### Type 1 diabetes

- Insulin dosing around exercise is the diabetes team's domain; aerobic exercise tends to lower
  glucose, intense/anaerobic work can raise it. Coach builds a consistent routine and logs
  sessions/readings for the diabetes team. Refer nutrition (carb counting) to their team/RD.

### Obesity (excess weight)

- Weight-neutral, respectful language; focus on capability, health markers and habits as well as
  weight. Joint-friendly progressions (cycling, walking, step-ups, supported squats); heat
  tolerance and chafing; equipment weight limits. Realistic loss ~0.5–1% body weight per week.
  Ask about snoring/daytime sleepiness (sleep apnoea → GP), knee/back pain, medicines that cause
  weight gain, past dieting history. Resistance training protects muscle during loss.

### Fatty liver (MASLD/NAFLD)

- Common in India, including in people who aren't very overweight. Weight loss (~7–10%) improves
  liver fat; exercise helps even without weight loss; cut sugary drinks/fructose-heavy foods and
  alcohol. Liver enzymes and imaging are the doctor's. Avoid supplements with liver-injury reports.

### High cholesterol / triglycerides

- Exercise, weight loss, fibre (oats, isabgol, dals), fewer refined carbs/sugar (triglycerides),
  unsaturated fats in place of saturated; limit alcohol. Statin users: report new muscle pain.

### High uric acid / gout

- Hydration; limit alcohol (especially beer), sugary drinks and very large meat/organ-meat
  portions; avoid crash dieting and long fasts (can trigger flares); dairy and plant protein are
  fine. During a flare: rest the joint, doctor manages treatment.

## Cardiovascular

### Hypertension

- **Ask:** usual readings, medicines, home monitoring, dizziness on standing, doctor limits.
- **Training:** aerobic, dynamic resistance, combined and isometric training all lower resting BP;
  isometric work (e.g. wall squats, handgrip) gave the largest reductions in a 270-RCT network
  meta-analysis (Edwards 2023). Steady breathing — no breath-holding/Valsalva; avoid maximal lifts
  and long head-down positions if uncontrolled; longer cool-down (post-exercise dizziness with BP
  medicines). Beta-blockers → use RPE/talk test.
- **Nutrition:** DASH-style eating; reduce salt (WHO: < 5 g salt/day) — pickles, papad, namkeen,
  packaged and restaurant food are the big sources; potassium-rich foods (fruit, veg, dals)
  _unless_ kidney disease or potassium-raising medicines; limit alcohol; weight loss.
- **Watch for:** see triage C/D thresholds (safety-screening.md).
- **Myths:** "lifting weights is dangerous with high BP" (controlled resistance training is
  recommended); "BP medicine means no exercise".

### Known heart disease (heart attack, stents, bypass, heart failure, arrhythmia)

- Triage C: needs cardiology/cardiac-rehab guidance on intensity before structured training. Once
  cleared: RPE-guided, gradual, longer warm-up/cool-down, symptoms checklist each session.

## Endocrine

### Hypothyroidism / Hashimoto's

- **Ask:** diagnosis and when; levothyroxine dose changes; last review (don't interpret TSH);
  fatigue, cold intolerance, constipation, low mood; doctor restrictions.
- **Training:** once treated and stable, train normally with gradual progression. Under-treatment
  can cause fatigue and slower progress — note it and suggest a review rather than pushing harder.
- **Nutrition:** normal healthy diet; take levothyroxine as prescribed (empty stomach) and keep
  calcium, iron, magnesium, soy and high-fibre remedies away from it per the doctor/pharmacist;
  cooked cruciferous veg (cabbage, cauliflower) in normal amounts are fine; iodised salt is the
  usual iodine source — no kelp/iodine supplements without a doctor (excess iodine can worsen
  thyroid problems).
- **Myths:** "thyroid diet", "never eat cabbage/soy", "gluten-free fixes Hashimoto's" (no strong
  evidence unless coeliac disease — a Decision-Brief item if the client wants it), "thyroid makes
  fat loss impossible" (treated hypothyroidism has a modest effect; most weight change is
  energy balance).

### Hyperthyroidism / Graves'

- Uncontrolled: fast heart rate, tremor, heat intolerance, weight loss, palpitations → triage C:
  only light activity until the doctor says it is controlled. Beta-blockers → RPE. Bone density
  and muscle loss → resistance training once controlled. After treatment (radioiodine/surgery),
  many become hypothyroid — follow the hypothyroid notes. Avoid stimulant pre-workouts.

### PCOS

- **Ask:** main concerns (cycles, weight, skin/hair, fertility, mood), medicines (metformin, pill),
  eating patterns (higher rates of disordered eating), mood.
- **Training:** any physical activity helps; follow general adult guidelines (aerobic + muscle-
  strengthening ≥ 2 days/week) per the 2023 international guideline; resistance training supports
  insulin sensitivity and body composition.
- **Nutrition:** no specific diet is proven superior; a healthy, sustainable pattern with a modest
  deficit if weight loss is a goal (5–10% loss can improve symptoms); protein + fibre; avoid
  unnecessary bans (dairy, gluten) without a reason.
- **Myths:** "PCOS = impossible fat loss", "dairy/gluten cause PCOS", "inositol cures PCOS"
  (evidence limited — remedies file).
- **Clinician:** diagnosis, medicines, fertility, mental health support.

## Women's health

### Pregnancy

- The obstetric care provider leads. For uncomplicated pregnancies, ACOG (2020) supports regular
  moderate activity (~150 min/week). Avoid contact/fall-risk sports, scuba, hot yoga; avoid
  prolonged lying flat on the back later in pregnancy; no new weight-loss diets. Stop and seek care
  for warning signs (safety-screening.md §3). Coach only within the provider's guidance.

### Postpartum (incl. diastasis recti, pelvic floor, breastfeeding)

- **Ask:** time since birth; vaginal or caesarean; complications; postnatal check done/restrictions;
  breastfeeding; sleep; mood; leaking/heaviness/pain; abdominal doming or gap concerns.
- **Training:** gradual return as soon as medically safe (ACOG 2020); start with walking,
  breathing, pelvic-floor training, gentle core and full-body strength; progress by symptoms
  (no leaking, heaviness, pain or doming) rather than the calendar. Running and impact usually
  later (a widely used return-to-running guideline suggests not before ~12 weeks, after
  assessment). Abdominal exercises are not forbidden with diastasis — choose a level the client
  can control without doming or symptoms.
- **Nutrition:** breastfeeding raises energy needs (roughly +330–400 kcal/day in the first months);
  avoid aggressive deficits — gradual loss (~0.5 kg/week) is generally compatible with milk
  supply; hydration; iron, calcium, B12, vitamin D as advised by the doctor.
- **Watch for:** pelvic-floor symptoms, doming that doesn't improve, wound problems, heavy
  bleeding, low mood/anxiety (postpartum depression → GP; Tele-MANAS 14416). Postpartum thyroiditis
  can cause palpitations then fatigue.
- **Myths:** "crunches forever banned", "flat belly in 6 weeks", "spot-reduce the mummy tummy",
  "breastfeeding burns all the baby weight".
- **Referral:** pelvic-health physiotherapist for symptoms; obstetrician for complications.

### Peri-menopause & menopause

- Resistance training (progressive, including heavier loads when appropriate) for muscle and bone;
  impact/jumping if suitable for bone; balance work; protein ~1.2–1.6 g/kg; sleep and hot-flush
  strategies (cool room, layering, alcohol/caffeine timing). Weight redistribution to the abdomen
  is common — no "menopause belly" fat burners. HRT and symptoms are the doctor's domain.

## Musculoskeletal

### Low back pain (non-specific)

- Most back pain is non-specific; staying active and exercise therapy are first-line (NICE NG59).
  Use tolerated movements, progress gradually, vary posture; no fear language ("weak core caused
  this", "never bend"). Red flags → urgent (cauda equina signs, fever, unexplained weight loss,
  cancer history, major trauma, night pain); leg pain below the knee with numbness/weakness →
  physio/doctor.

### Knee pain / osteoarthritis

- Exercise (strength + aerobic) and weight management are core treatment in OA guidelines.
  Pain-monitoring rule many physios use: pain up to ~3–5/10 during exercise is acceptable if it
  settles by the next morning and isn't increasing week to week. Box squats to a comfortable height,
  step-ups, leg press, hip strengthening, cycling. Locking, giving way, big swelling after injury,
  hot red joint → doctor/physio.

### Shoulder pain

- Train in pain-free ranges; reduce overhead loading; build rowing/rotator-cuff strength;
  physio for persistent pain, night pain, weakness or after dislocation.

### After surgery / recent injury

- Follow the surgeon's/physio's protocol and timelines; the coach trains everything else and
  reinforces home exercises — not rehab. Get written restrictions.

### Osteoporosis / low bone density

- Progressive resistance and (if suitable) impact training improve bone; supervised high-intensity
  resistance training has been used safely in post-menopausal women with low bone mass (LIFTMOR
  trial). Avoid fast/loaded spinal flexion and twisting ("Too Fit To Fracture" recommendations);
  balance work for falls; calcium/vitamin D/protein; medicines are the doctor's.

## Other

### Anaemia & vitamin deficiencies (iron, B12, vitamin D)

- Fatigue and breathlessness limit training — keep intensity moderate until treated, then
  progress. Treatment doses are the doctor's. Diet: iron-rich foods with vitamin C, tea/coffee away
  from meals, B12 sources for vegetarians (dairy, eggs, fortified foods).

### Digestive issues

- Constipation: fibre gradually, water, activity, routine. Acidity/GERD: smaller dinners, earlier,
  no lying down for 2–3 h, identify personal triggers. Bloating: slow fibre/protein-powder changes,
  check lactose (curd/hung curd/paneer usually tolerated). IBS: low-FODMAP only with a dietitian.
  Blood in stool, weight loss, persistent change in bowel habit, difficulty swallowing → doctor.

### Migraine

- Regular meals, sleep, hydration; gradual warm-ups; avoid heat/strong sun and very cold AC if
  triggers; no training during an attack; log migraine days. Sudden "worst ever" headache or a new
  pattern → doctor (urgent if sudden/severe).

### Asthma

- Reliever as prescribed (pre-exercise use per doctor); long warm-ups; avoid very cold, dry or
  polluted outdoor air — **train indoors on high-AQI days** (relevant in many Indian cities in
  winter); stop and use the reliever if wheezy.

### Kidney disease

- Protein, potassium, phosphorus and fluid targets are set by the nephrologist/RD. Avoid creatine,
  high-dose supplements, potassium-rich remedies, salt substitutes. Activity per doctor.

### Depression, anxiety, stress

- Exercise reduces symptoms of depression and anxiety (strong evidence), alongside — never instead
  of — treatment. Make the plan small and flexible; celebrate attendance. Crisis → Tele-MANAS 14416
  / emergency 112.

### Possible disordered eating

- Signs: rigid rules, guilt, bingeing, compensating (vomiting, laxatives, over-exercise), intense
  fear of weight gain, very low intake. Don't prescribe deficits, calorie tracking or frequent
  weighing; focus on regular meals and enjoyable movement; suggest professional support kindly.

### ADHD / neurodivergence

- Short, simple, visual plans; one daily tick; reminders and fixed slots; variety within a stable
  structure; Busy Day fallback. Stimulant medicines may suppress appetite → protein at breakfast
  and evening.

### Older adults

- Multicomponent training (balance + strength + aerobic) ≥ 3 days/week for falls prevention (WHO
  2020); protein ~1.0–1.2 g/kg or more if active; chair-supported progressions; check medicines
  (BP, diabetes, blood thinners), joint replacements, vision.

### GLP-1 medicines (semaglutide, tirzepatide) and other weight-loss medicines

- Rapid weight loss can include significant muscle loss → resistance training 2–3×/week and
  protein at each meal are priorities; small, protein-first meals for low appetite/nausea;
  hydration; constipation management; hypos only with insulin/sulfonylureas. Dose and stopping are
  the doctor's decisions; plan for maintenance habits for when/if the medicine stops.
